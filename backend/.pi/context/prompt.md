# Bursar — domain exploration intent

## One sentence

Bursar is a delegated bill-paying agent for a household: an owner gives an AI agent a mandate
to pay a parent's recurring household bills, while a machine-enforced constitution and an
on-chain second lock make it impossible for the agent — or a stolen operator key — to pay the
wrong payee, exceed the caps, or pay the same invoice twice.

## The problem

An adult child manages a parent's household bills. The parent should not have to think about
due dates, portals, or crypto. The child wants to delegate the work without handing over
unlimited authority. The failure modes are concrete:

- a scam invoice says "our bank details changed" and redirects a payment;
- an agent (or a prompt injection) splits one payment into several to evade a limit;
- a payment is approved, then the payee is frozen before dispatch;
- the same invoice is paid twice after a timeout;
- a stolen operator key drains the account;
- nobody can prove afterwards what was allowed, what was refused, and why.

## Personas

- Owner: the adult child. Holds the money and the rules. Approves exceptions. Uses a phone.
- Payer: the parent. Receives the service. No crypto knowledge. Never signs anything.
- Agent: software. Proposes payments. Never holds keys, never edits policy.
- Payees: utility, insurer, pharmacy, home service, dentist.
- Adversary: scammer or prompt injector. Changes bank details, splits payments, replays invoices.

## Product principles

1. The agent proposes; the code disposes.
2. Rules are operator-owned and uneditable by the agent.
3. Approval binds to the exact payment, not to a screen.
4. Authority is re-read at the moment of consequence.
5. Every attempt leaves evidence, including refusals.
6. Crypto is the settlement rail and the second lock, not the pitch.
7. No LLM in the enforcement path.

## Scope

In: invoices, payees, mandate/constitution, payment attempts, approvals, authority
determinations, USDC settlement, evidence, one household, USDC only, Base Sepolia and a local
Anvil fallback.

Out: trading, speculation, price prediction, lending, multi-tenant SaaS, real bank rails, tax,
accounting integration, cross-chain, fiat on/off-ramp (the bank payout is simulated and labeled).

## Proposed bounded contexts (challenge these)

- mandate — owner, payer, agent, payees, constitution versions
- billing — invoices, payment attempts, agent proposals
- authority — payment intents, determinations, reservations
- settlement — chain adapter, receipts, nullifier reconciliation
- evidence — envelopes, verification, on-chain anchors
- console — API/UI composition, read models; no business rules

## Ubiquitous language

owner, payer, agent, mandate, constitution, payee, invoice, bank digest, payment intent,
payment attempt, approval, determination, reservation, settlement, receipt, evidence envelope,
anchor, quarantine, freeze, cap, threshold.

## Key invariants

- No payment without a determination at dispatch time.
- A reservation is atomic; committed + reserved never exceeds the daily cap.
- An invoice settles at most once, on-chain.
- A bank-detail change quarantines the payee until the owner re-approves.
- A new payee or a payment above threshold requires an owner signature bound to the exact intent.
- A refusal never produces a side effect.
- Every attempt has evidence; refusals are first-class.

## Scenarios (acceptance)

1. A normal bill auto-pays; USDC settles; the receipt verifies.
2. A poisoned invoice changes bank details; refused at plan time; no transaction.
3. An invoice is approved, then the payee is frozen; refused at dispatch; no transaction.
4. Six split invoices; the aggregate daily cap refuses the excess; earlier ones settle.
5. A new payee or large amount escalates; the owner signs EIP-712; it settles; a wrong
   signature is refused.
6. A duplicate invoice bypasses the app; the contract reverts.
7. Evidence: the envelope verifies; one flipped byte fails; the hash is anchored on-chain.

## Technical constraints (do not invent alternatives)

- Governance: Rigorix OSS `rigorix-server` (unmodified) with the Python SDK client:
  execute, approve, audit.read.
- Enforcement: `.rigorix/preconditions.toml`, `.rigorix/sequence-policy.toml`,
  `.rigorix/capacity.toml`; approval binding; signed envelopes.
- Payment step: `run_command` with a fixed `command` path plus structured params:
  intent_id, invoice_id, payee, payee_address, amount_usdc, bank_digest, is_new_payee,
  requires_owner_signature. The command is generated by code, never by the LLM.
- Authority: a separate FastAPI service with SQLite; the precondition program is a thin
  client outside the agent-writable workspace, invoked by Rigorix at dispatch.
- Settlement: Python web3/eth-account against `PaymentRouter.sol` (Foundry), USDC on Base
  Sepolia; Anvil fallback.
- Approval: EIP-712 typed data, verified off-chain by the authority and on-chain by the contract.
- Evidence: HMAC envelope verified with the Python SDK and `rigorix-verifier/browser`;
  envelope hash anchored on-chain.
- Host language: Python. Rust only exists as the OSS server. Guardian target language: Python.
- Frontend: a separate React/TypeScript/Vite app, API-first, built in parallel against mocks.

## Honest boundaries

- Rigorix enforces the operator's determination; it does not know what "payee eligible" means.
- HMAC is operator-domain tamper evidence; the on-chain anchor adds independent integrity and time.
- A precondition narrows the check-to-consequence window; the contract is the commit-point lock.
- Local Anvil is a demo convenience; Base Sepolia is the live claim.
- The bank payout is simulated and labeled as such.
